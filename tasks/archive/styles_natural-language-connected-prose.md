# Refine the natural-language output style toward connected, balanced prose and ship its behavioural eval harness on a shared worker-isolation helper

## Goal

Rewrite `styles/natural-language.md` so text written under it reads as connected, balanced prose on the first read. Sentences keep the relations between claims, paragraphs lead with their point and hold one theme, enumerations appear as lists or as a colon series, and sentence length follows what each sentence carries. The style keeps its existing strengths, which are plain verbs, exact terms, spelled-out abbreviations, and text free of padding and worn phrases, and chat answers stay concise under the Approach's **`chat_brevity` scenario.** bar. The change ships with a behavioural eval harness under `tests/natural_language/` that proves both halves: a rewrite keeps its relations and its shape, and a chat answer stays concise. The harness runs every worker and judge in a sandbox isolated from the host, through a shared helper under `tests/lib/` that this task builds first so that other behavioural runners can import it.

## Context

- **What the style regulates today.** Its rules work almost entirely at sentence level, and the most checkable ones all push toward short, separate sentences:
  - `<short_sentences>`: "Keep most sentences under about twenty words. Split a longer one into two."
  - `<one_idea>`: "Give each sentence one idea, and say it once."
  - `<ordinary_joins>` ends with "Prefer a full stop where a colon or semicolon would also work."
  - `<parentheticals>`: "Give a qualification that matters its own sentence, and cut one that does not. Keep main clauses free of interrupting parentheticals."
  - The validation "Most sentences run under about twenty words, and each carries one idea."

  The counterweights, `<complete_sentences>` keeping connectives and `<ordinary_joins>` naming conjunctions, act inside one sentence only, and no validation checks how consecutive sentences relate. The `<long_tail>` test, "A phrase that would survive unchanged in a document on another topic carries no content", also catches connectives such as "so", "but" and "on top of that". No rule covers paragraphs, and the length rule discourages the subordinate clauses that would show which point matters most. The list rule in `<policy>` reserves lists for "a genuine enumeration" without a test for what counts as one. `<plain_layout>`, `<answer_first>` and the `<format>` line address chat answers, although `<scope>` includes documentation. `<definitions>` ("at first use") and `<report_once>` ("Trust the reader to carry it forward") assume a reader who reads from the top, and `<goal_statements>` asks what, who and how the reader will know, but not what gets built.
- **The failure this produces.** Text written under the style reads flat. It runs as short single-clause statements with a new subject in almost every sentence, few connectives, sentences chained with "It…", and enumerations written as runs of sentences. The logic between sentences goes missing, every sentence carries the same weight, the causal chain stays implicit, conclusions land at the end of paragraphs, and dense paragraphs turn into walls of text. A rewrite of a roughly 1,600-word argumentative document under the current style illustrates the scale: the sentence count rose from 65 to 101 and the word count by 13%, the mean sentence fell from 24 to 18 words, the standard deviation of sentence length narrowed from 15 words to 8, and the sentences opening with It, They, This or Its tripled from 4 to 12. A hand-edited version of the same material ran at a mean of 24 words with a standard deviation of 19, and restoring the dropped content afterwards left the flat shape unchanged. The mechanisms behind those numbers:
  - A split drops a relation. "A migration makes the schema a contract, but whether every client honours it still rests on each client." becomes two sentences without the "but".
  - Splits replace subordination with pronoun chains. "It works because A, and because B." becomes "It works for two reasons." followed by one sentence per reason, and "The service, deployed last month, handles every login." becomes "The service was deployed last month. It handles every login."
  - The full-stop preference turns a series into prose. "three stages: parsing, validation and storage" becomes "One stage parses… A second validates… The third stores…".
  - The vocabulary rules work as intended: abbreviations get spelled out, and internal shorthand gives way to names a reader knows.
- **Related work.** [ai-editorial_language-humanizer-skill.md](ai-editorial_language-humanizer-skill.md) words several of the same moves for a skill: transitions carry the logic, connected prose stays prose, parallel points become lists, and causal joints enter a ledger. The Approach's style-rewrite items already carry the wording that aligns this style with that skill's overlapping moves, so the repo states one model of readable prose; this task does not require a live diff against `plugins/ai_editorial/skills/language_humanizer/SKILL.md`, which the sibling task still owns and has not shipped. Its harness under `tests/language_humanizer/`, with its runner, deterministic grader, refute-biased judge, fixed-denominator measurement and `compression_trap` fixture, is the model for this task's harness.
- **How a headless worker loads a style and what else reaches it.** Probes of Claude Code 2.1.226 on 2026-10-02 showed that a `claude -p` worker loads a style staged in the sandbox's `.claude/output-styles/` and selected by `outputStyle` in the sandbox's `.claude/settings.json`, also under `--setting-sources project,local`, and that a malformed sandbox `settings.json` drops the selection without an error. They also showed that only a sandbox outside the home directory and outside any repository, under that flag, keeps the user-level settings, a deployed style and every host CLAUDE.md away from the worker. Those host files carry writing rules of their own, so isolation matters for this eval. The findings are recorded under `### A headless worker honours a project selection` on `wiki/concepts/claude-output-style-selection.md`, under `### A worker inherits the host's instructions unless it is isolated` on `wiki/concepts/verification-surfaces.md`, and under `### The two layers` on `wiki/concepts/claude-output-styles.md` for `--append-system-prompt-file`, which appends a style body beside the style layer rather than in it.
- **Shared test wiring and the Cursor limits.** `tests/lib/` holds the helpers several runners share, such as `vendor.py` for the worker abstraction, and its standalone unit tests follow the pattern of `tests/lib/test_worker_io.py` and `tests/lib/test_vendor.py`. `TESTING.md` prefers `--vendor cursor` for behavioural runs, so an isolation helper there serves Cursor runners as well as Claude ones. For a Cursor worker started with `agent -p`, the isolated root is the surface a helper can isolate. Two Cursor sources stay outside that reach: User Rules, which Cursor keeps in its settings rather than in project files (`### The user rule is not a file` on `wiki/entities/cursor.md`), and deployed user-level skills (`### Agent definitions` on the same page, from a probe on 4 October 2026).
- **Constraints.** The output-style frontmatter schema declares four keys and the loader ignores any other, as recorded under `### Frontmatter` on `wiki/concepts/claude-output-styles.md`, so the style keeps its current keys. The standing repo rules for AI-consumed instruction files govern the file's structure and wording. The pseudo-XML linter that ships with `ai_instruction_formatting` passes on the current file.

## Approach

Rewrite each passage below in place to its target form, so one canonical statement remains per rule. The proposed wording fixes each rule's substance, and its phrasing may adapt to the file's voice and to the standing wording rules.

1. **Lead with connection in the frontmatter `description` and in `<objective>`.** These two set the frame every later rule is read in. Description: "Write plain, connected prose in a natural voice. Answer first, use the precise technical term, link each sentence to the last, and stop when the answer is delivered." Objective: "Write like one person explaining something to another. Use plain words and the exact term, let sentence length follow what each sentence carries, and build each sentence on the one before, so the reader follows the reasoning on the first read. When restyling existing text, keep every source relation, open each passage with its point, gloss every term at first use, open every calculation with its conclusion, and finish shorter than the source. In a chat answer, answer the question, then stop."
2. **Let `<short_sentences>` follow content.** "A plain fact can take ten words, and a claim with its reason, condition or contrast can take twenty-five to thirty-five when a conjunction joins them. Split where a sentence stacks a second claim or a second subordinate clause." Replace the validation "Most sentences run under about twenty words, and each carries one idea." with "Sentence lengths vary, and every main clause is clear on the first read."
3. **Narrow `<one_idea>` and make every split carry its relation.** "Give each sentence one claim, and keep its reason, condition or contrast in the same sentence when a conjunction can carry it. When you split a sentence, carry the relation into the next one with 'But', 'So' or 'That is why'." Add `<keep_the_relations>` to `<keep_the_meaning>`: "Keep every relation the source states: cause, contrast, condition, consequence and concession. When restyling existing text, note its relations before the edit and check each one after. Keep each relation's class and its marker: write a cause with because or since, and write a contrast with but or yet in the same sentence the source used. When the source joins two claims with but or yet, keep that word in that sentence, and keep who is affected. Swap but for and still, or turn the second claim into a counterfactual such as 'without X, Y would', only when the source itself used that shape. When the source holds for two reasons with because …, and because …, keep both because markers; rewrite those causes as while, until or as only when the source itself used a circumstance marker. When a relation has several members, keep every member at the source's specificity, including who is affected and what still fails or still passes." Add the validation "Every relation the source stated is still stated, including every member of a multi-member relation, and a source contrast joined by but or yet still uses that word in that sentence."
4. **Let `<ordinary_joins>` use colons.** Delete "Prefer a full stop where a colon or semicolon would also work." and add: "Use a colon when the first clause sets up a list or an explanation that the second delivers, and keep a full clause before it. A fragment before a colon, as in 'The result: …', reads as a headline." `<dash_replacement>` stays as it is, because it targets a real tell, and the colon restores one of the relation-carrying marks the dash ban removes.
5. **Relax `<parentheticals>` for short glosses.** "Gloss a term or list its parts inline with a short appositive or parenthesis, as in 'a canary release, one that reaches a small share of users first' or '(owner, deadline, budget)'. Give a longer qualification its own sentence." Replace the validation "No main clause is interrupted by a parenthetical." with "No aside longer than a few words breaks a main clause."
6. **Add a `<paragraphs>` section after `<sentence_style>`,** scoped to documents and other multi-paragraph text, with one element per rule:
   - `<one_theme>`: "Give each paragraph one theme, and open it with its point, then the evidence, then what the evidence means for the point. Open an explanatory paragraph with its verdict or claim, such as that the rollout is incomplete until every region has cut over, and put the mechanism that supports that claim after it. In a paragraph that carries figures or a calculation, the point is the conclusion those numbers support, so write that conclusion first and put the numbers after it. A topic heading names the section; the first sentence of the body still states the point."
   - `<section_lead>`: "Lead a section with the conclusion or controlling fact it rests on. Open a calculation passage with what the numbers establish, such as that the budget is already spent, then show the line items. State a key caveat early, as a claim."
   - `<concrete_actor>`: "Keep one subject through a paragraph where the argument allows, and prefer a concrete actor such as the user, the team or we over an abstract one such as the project or it."
   - `<known_to_new>`: "Start a sentence with what the reader already has, and end it with what is new."
   - `<mark_the_relation>`: "Mark how a sentence relates to the one before whenever the reader could miss it, with because, so, but or which means. When a figure supports a claim or decision, say so in that passage, for example that the latency drop is what justifies the cutover."
   - `<clear_referents>`: "Give 'it', 'this' and 'they' a referent in the previous sentence."
   - `<split_the_block>`: "Split a block that carries two themes or a run of figures, and keep only the figures the conclusion needs. When rewriting, finish under the source length: cut repeated setup and restated framing before you keep any optional colour, and preserve every relation."

   Add the validations "Each paragraph opens with its point and holds one theme, even under a topic heading. An explanatory paragraph opens with its verdict, and a passage with figures or a calculation opens with the conclusion those numbers support.", "A restyled document finishes shorter than its source, with repeated setup and previewed conclusions cut before any required relation.", and "No three sentences in a row open with It, This, They or Its."
7. **Turn the list `<rule>` in `<policy>` into a self-describing `<lists>` element that carries a test.** "Set three or more parallel items of one kind, such as checks, causes, signals or steps, as a list after a lead-in sentence, or as a series after a colon. Label an item that has a name, and give a risk, its test and its fallback separate labels. Keep prose where the transitions carry the argument." Add the validation "Every enumeration of three or more parallel items is a list or a colon series."
8. **Separate chat answers from documents.** Rewrite `<plain_layout>` as: "In a chat answer, write prose, with a list or table for an enumeration or a comparison. In a document, use the structure its type prescribes, such as a template's sections, a fixed form like an if/then/because hypothesis, headings, labels and lists. Lead each section with its point, keep it at the level of detail its job needs, and leave mechanisms to the section that owns them." Rewrite the `<format>` line as: "In a chat answer, write plain connected prose, answer first, let sentence length follow what each sentence carries, and stop when the answer is delivered. No headings." Rewrite `<answer_first>` as: "In a chat answer, put the answer in the first sentence. Add only what the reader needs after it." Rewrite `<answer_what_was_asked>` as: "In a chat answer, answer the question that was asked. Leave out background, alternatives, and history the reader did not ask for." Rewrite `<cut_the_padding>` as: "In a chat answer, skip preambles, restatements of the request, previews of the answer, and closing summaries. Skip narration of work the tool calls already show. Leave out any list of what you chose not to do." Rewrite `<stop_early>` as: "In a chat answer, most answers need a few sentences. Write those, then stop. Content the reader asked for is what earns extra length." Rewrite the validation "The answer is in the first sentence." as: "In a chat answer, the answer is in the first sentence." Rewrite the validation "Nothing remains that the reader did not ask for." as: "In a chat answer, nothing remains that the reader did not ask for." Rewrite the validation "No preamble, restatement, preview, or closing recap appears." as: "In a chat answer, no preamble, restatement, preview, or closing recap appears." Give documents the point-first lead instead. Extend `<goal_statements>` with "Name what will be built or done before what it achieves."
9. **Exempt connectives in `<long_tail>`.** "Apply this test only to phrases that state neither a fact nor a relation. Connectives such as because, so, but, on top of that and which is why appear in every document because they carry the relation between claims, so keep them."
10. **Name the structural tells.** Add `<structural_tells>` to `<worn_phrasing>`, listing the tells the old sentence rules produce: uniform sentence rhythm, chains of pronoun-led sentences, fragment-colon headlines, rhythmic triplets, and "This means…" openers that point at nothing in particular.
11. **Fit `<definitions>` and `<report_once>` to documents read in pieces.** Rewrite `<definitions>` as: "Put a term's definition with its first use: as a short appositive or parenthesis in that sentence, or in the next sentence when that sentence's job is to define the term. When the source defines a term only later, move the gloss up to that first-use window, or introduce the idea in plain words and name the term only when the gloss is ready. Gloss it again at its first use in any section a reader may open on its own. Treat a bare 'the X' used as if known, such as 'the checks' or 'the method', as a term." Narrow `<report_once>` to a passage: "State a fact, a caveat or a limitation once within a passage. When restyling, leave each section's conclusion in that section rather than previewing it earlier." Add the validation "Every term and every 'the X' has a referent at first use in each section, in that sentence or in the next sentence when that sentence defines it."

Every element the items above do not name stays as it is. That covers the vocabulary rules (`<precise_terms>`, `<preserved_names>`, `<in_group_shorthand>`, `<abbreviations>`), the worn-phrase list (`<plain_verbs>`, `<retired_phrases>`), `<active_voice>`, `<verbs_over_nouns>`, `<full_meaning>`, `<dash_replacement>` and `<hyphen_role>`, which already work as intended.
